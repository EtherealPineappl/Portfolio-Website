// OVERVIEW

A metaphysical puzzle game where you solve your own murder after reincarnating as a rock. Built in Unreal Engine 5 in one week for the Unreal MegaJam 2022, earning the Kitbasher Award — a jury prize from the Epic Games team — out of 500+ entries. MetaHumans and motion capture bring the characters to life.

// KEY_RESPONSIBILITIES

RAPID GAME DEVELOPMENT

Designed, built, and shipped a complete game in one week under jam constraints, as Point Link Force with Benjamin Brown. Scoped the concept to a puzzle loop that was weird enough to stand out and finished enough to actually play.

METAHUMAN & MOTION CAPTURE

Integrated Unreal Engine 5's MetaHuman system for character creation and drove the faces with Live Link facial capture, with body animation from Mixamo.

PUZZLE & LEVEL DESIGN

Built the levels, designed and tuned the physics puzzles, and developed physics-based movement for a protagonist who is, technically, a rock.

// DESIGN_NOTES

AS ABOVE, SO BELOW

The jam's theme was "As above, so below": the idea that the microcosm of the individual mirrors the macrocosm of the universe. I took that literally. You die and cross over to a spiritual plane (the macrocosm), where you get the chance to solve your own murder ghost-style. The catch is that the only thing you can control is a rock, the micro-est of cosms.

BUILT AROUND THE KIT

We used a Kitbash3D cyberpunk city kit, so I adapted the story to fit it: a cyberpunk-esque detective world where the rock works alongside a detective to uncover its own death. The game won the jam's KitBasher award from the Epic Games team.

PHYSICS PUZZLES

Every puzzle was physics-based, which meant a lot of fine tuning to get each one working as intended. In one, you roll an office chair around the room so you can use it to get up onto the desk. I had to set up the chair's physics constraints so it felt real to push around and was still possible to jump on.

FIRST CINEMATICS

This was my first time using motion capture and Unreal's cinematic tools. I'm proud of what we got into a week: the art, the MetaHumans, the voice lines and the story.

WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY NOW

I focused so much on the cinematics and story that the puzzles fell behind and shipped unpolished. Some instructions weren't clear, and some of the physics puzzles could soft-lock the player. The puzzles weren't planned out; I adjusted them until they worked, which isn't how I design puzzles or levels today.

I also skipped greyboxing and placed final assets straight into the levels, so every large layout change took far too long. Now I start with a sketch, design markup and a greybox before any art goes in. The Axima Ruins breakdown shows that process.
